# Absolute location and relative relocation QC: correctness criteria and anomaly attribution

Scope: how seismologists judge whether absolute hypocentres (including the choice of velocity model) and relative relocations (double-difference, cluster-based) are correct, and whether an obvious anomaly can be attributed to a cause. Covers formal uncertainty vs accuracy, network-geometry criteria (GAP, secondary gap, nearest station, S within 1.4 focal depths), origin-time–depth trade-off, phase misidentification, velocity-model errors and their signatures (depth offsets, pile-ups at interfaces, trial or bound depths, station residuals and station terms), the minimum 1-D model, 1-D vs 3-D bias, station elevation and depth datum, probabilistic location (NonLinLoc, EDT vs L2, maximum-likelihood vs expectation), locator comparisons, synthetic tests, and for relocation: linkage, conditioning, error estimation (bootstrap, jackknife, SVD), cross-correlation data quality, centroid shifts, airquakes, streaks and cluster collapse, and validation by independent methods and structural plausibility.

Date: 2026-10-31. Stage: location and relocation of the reference-free checker (docs/research/02_Checker_Design.md, stages 4–5). Downloads are in the session scratchpad (lit_qc/location), not in the repository.

## 1. Correctness criteria

Our checks for these stages: location: events_outside_network (report-only), isolated_events, wadati_origin_offset (report-only), wadati_origin_spread, travel_time_intercept (report-only), azimuthal_gap (report-only), depth_single_value, depth_at_edges, depth_layers, sp_depth_offset, station_residual_outliers / station_residual_azimuth (report-only), model_ensemble_depth / model_ensemble_time (report-only); relocation: relocated_share, relocation_shift, relocation_large_moves, relocation_thinning; plus the dt.cc summary (pairs, observations per pair, CC quantiles; reported, not a check). "MISSING" = an expert criterion with no counterpart.

| L15 | No magnitude-dependent location offset | Large events are recorded farther and sample the model deeper; if model accuracy varies with depth they are offset from small events | No number given | Husen & Hardebeck 2010 | MISSING: depth/epicentre offset vs magnitude or vs maximum station distance |
| L16 | Lateral contrast bias absent or accounted for | Across a fault juxtaposing fast and slow rock, a 1-D model pulls events toward the fast side, more with depth, so a vertical fault can appear dipping | Remedy: station-group models (Oppenheimer et al. 1993), 3-D model | Husen & Hardebeck 2010 [Thurber 1992 via H&H] | MISSING (not reference-free; station_residual_azimuth is a weak proxy) |
| L17 | Validation with known sources | Relocate explosions, blasts, GT events with the chosen model; the model is only as good as its picks | Tunnel explosion: regional 3-D model 2 km too deep (truth inside the error region), local velocities ~20 % slower → 200 m; velocities varied ~10 % between stations 2–12 km away; "a 3-D velocity model does not necessarily yield more accurate locations" | Husen & Hardebeck 2010 | MISSING (needs external truth; quarry blasts or mine events in a case could serve) |

## 2. Attribution: anomaly → causes → discriminating evidence

Reliability scale: high = one test on the run's own data separates the causes; medium = needs an extra computation we can do reference-free (re-run with a perturbed model, grid, datum or locator); low = confounded without external truth (GT events, 3-D model, independent catalog).

## 3. Key insights for an automated checker

Reliable automatically (exact or near-exact signatures, decidable from the run's own data and configuration):
1. Default, trial, fixed and bound depths repeat a value exactly; matching pile depths against the run's own trial depth, grid nodes, grid bounds, datum and model interfaces names the cause. depth_single_value and depth_at_edges should report which of these the value coincides with.
2. A datum mismatch is a constant offset of known size (station elevation, model-top elevation); it is separable from model errors if the run states its depth reference and whether elevations were used. A required "depth reference" field plus a residual-vs-elevation regression would make this decidable.
3. Depth control is a per-event property (close S within ~1.4 z, nearest station, GAP and secondary gap). Stratifying every depth check by it is the single most useful addition: artefact pile-ups are dominated by unconstrained events, real structure persists among constrained events.
4. Relocation health indicators are mechanical: linkage (≥ 8 observations per pair, cluster count and sizes), residual reduction per data type, data kept after re-weighting, airquakes removed, centroid shift compared with initial errors.

Needs a re-run, but then reliable (perturbation tests; cost minutes):
5. Interface pile-ups: shift interfaces, use the gradient version, refine the grid; a pile that follows is an artefact. This turns our step-vs-gradient finding into a test; which version is better must be decided by data fit (joint RMS with station terms) per region, not by a rule, because the better model is the one closer to the local profile (Yu et al.).
6. Robustness of relocations: bootstrap (B ≈ 50–100), jackknife by station, start-position perturbation, catalog-only vs CC-only vs combined, and a second relocator. These are the literature's standard tools for judging whether a streak or collapse is real.
7. P-only vs P+S and EDT vs L2 location of a sample expose S misidentification and outlier-driven locations, which formal errors and RMS hide.

Needs analyst judgement or external information:
8. Whether a thin slice at an interface or a sharp lineation is geology (real layer, real fault) after it survives the perturbation tests; focal mechanisms and fault maps are the usual arbiters.
9. Lateral-contrast bias, apparent dips, and the absolute position of the whole catalog need a 3-D model or known sources; relative methods do not improve absolute accuracy (Menke & Schaff via H&H; Lin & Shearer).

Fundamentally ambiguous reference-free:
10. Absolute depth bias from the velocity model vs Vp/Vs vs systematic S-pick bias vs origin-time trade-off: all move depth in coupled ways, and the sign depends on network geometry. Only GT events, a validated model, or a synthetic recovery test with the actual station geometry separate them; the checker should report "consistent with model/Vp-Vs/S-pick bias" rather than name one.
11. Formal errors (single-event or LSQR) measure precision only; every locator in the synthetic Ridgecrest test under-covered the truth. Reported errors should never be used as evidence of accuracy, and lower errors after a change are not evidence of improvement.

Thresholds against the literature: sp_depth_offset 1.5 km matches Gomberg's ±1.5 km for depth with a close S. relocation_shift 1.5 km is reasonable horizontally but tight in depth for networks whose formal depth errors are 2–3 km; the literature criterion is "within the average error of the initial locations", which argues for an adaptive limit. relocated_share ≥ 50 % sits at the edge of a published good relocation (49 % at Spanish Springs with many M < 0.5 events); computing it above Mc or above a pick count would avoid penalising unrelocatable micro-events. relocation_thinning catches collapse but cannot tell repeaters from artefacts without CC and resampling information.

Most important MISSING checks, in order: (1) per-event depth-control class (close S within 1.4 z, nearest station, GAP, secondary gap) and stratified depth checks; (2) coincidence of pile-ups with trial depth, grid nodes/bounds, datum and interfaces, plus an interface/grid perturbation re-run; (3) depth-reference declaration and residual-vs-elevation test; (4) relocation linkage statistics and per-data-type residual reduction; (5) bootstrap or jackknife on a subset and a start-position test for relocations; (6) locator ensemble (same picks and model, NLL EDT vs L2 vs Hypoinverse); (7) P-only vs P+S control on a sample; (8) per-event PDF diagnostics for NLL (ML−expectation distance, grid-edge truncation).
