# QC of the data/preprocessing and phase-picking stages

**Question.** How do seismologists judge whether the waveform data, the preprocessing and the phase picks are correct? When an obvious anomaly appears in these stages, can it be traced to its cause reliably?

**Scope.** The data stage covers station metadata and instrument response (gain, sensitivity, poles and zeros, units), timing (GPS loss, clock drift and jumps), orientation and polarity, clipping, spikes, offsets, gaps, sample-rate consistency, filter phase, and noise-level QC systems (MUSTANG/ISPAQ, PSD-PDF with the Peterson NLNM/NHNM). The picks stage covers manual and automatic pick accuracy, how ML pickers fail (domain shift, picks on noise, S picked in the P coda, picks missed during aftershock overlap, systematic offsets), quality weighting, station residual analysis, Wadati diagrams, S/P counts and network practice. Location, association, magnitude and catalog-level checks are left to the sibling notes `anomaly_attribution.md` and `magnitude_and_catalog.md`.

**Date.** 2026-10-31. Literature review by a Claude subagent. Downloads are in the session scratchpad (`lit_qc/data_picking/`) and are not in the repository. No downloaded code was executed. The ISPAQ and IRISMustangMetrics source files were read as text.

**How much of each source was read.** Each source is tagged with one of four read levels:
- **[F]**: full text read.
- **[F-part]**: full text, with only the relevant sections read.
- **[A]**: abstract or landing page only.
- **[S]**: secondary. Known only through a source read in full (named in the row) or through a search snippet.

Four sources were read in full: Küperkoch, Meier & Diehl, NMSOP-2 Ch. 16; Münchmeyer et al. 2022; Stehly et al. 2007; and the metric code of MUSTANG/ISPAQ (nine metric functions). Ten sources were read in part: Hable et al. 2018, Naranjo et al. 2024, Lin, Shearer & Hauksson 2008, Hutton et al. 2010, Mousavi et al. 2020 (EQTransformer), Zhu & Beroza (PhaseNet preprint), Woollam et al. (Durrës preprint), Liu & Tan (Blanco OBS preprint), Bornstein et al. (PickBlue preprint) and Zhao et al. 2025. Lin & Shearer 2007 was skimmed. About 15 further sources are [A] or [S]. Numbers from [A] or [S] sources are marked as such in the tables, and §5 lists them for the user to download.

**Our checker, as of this date** (`seismo_checker/source/catalog_checker`).
- Data stage: stations_with_data, daily_station_count (low-day fraction 0.5), network_geometry, channel_sample_rates, data_gaps (channel-day coverage at least 0.9; at most 5 % of channel-days below that), processing_time_shift (|median lag| of processed vs zero-phase-filtered raw at most 0.1 s), and representative energetic windows.
- Picks stage: silent_stations (at most 10 %), station_day_gaps (at most 5 %), duplicate_picks (same station and phase within 0.3 s; at most 2 %), s_to_p_ratio (at least 0.3), pick_onset_offset (AIC onset vs pick, P only, at most 0.2 s, on picks with onset SNR of at least 3), pick_snr (report-only, low at 2), station residual outliers (0.5 s, at least 20 picks; report-only) and azimuthal residual pattern (0.25 s; report-only).

## 1. Correctness criteria

| # | Criterion | What is checked and how | Typical thresholds or numbers | Source (read level) | Our check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Data availability and gaps | Per channel-day: percent availability, number of gaps and maximum gap. MUSTANG metric categories are archive status, data statistics and continuity, signal anomalies, noise, metadata checks and state of health (SOH). | MUSTANG reports these values and sets no fixed pass threshold; a DMC analyst reviews them and writes monthly network reports. | Casey et al. 2018 [A]; MUSTANG/ISPAQ code [F] | data_gaps, daily_station_count, stations_with_data |
| D2 | Sample rate in data vs metadata | `sample_rate_channel` is a boolean: the miniSEED rate and the channel-metadata rate must agree within 1 %. | Tolerance 1 % | IRISMustangMetrics `sampleRateChannelMetric.R` [F] | channel_sample_rates (partial: internal consistency only, not against metadata) |
| D3 | Sample rate in data vs response | `sample_rate_resp` finds the FIR roll-off corner, taken as the steepest slope of at most −50 in the amplitude response, sets the response rate to 2 × corner, and flags a disagreement with the miniSEED rate beyond 15 %. A mismatch shows that the attached response belongs to a different data stream. | Tolerance 15 % | `sampleRateRespMetric.R` [F] | MISSING |
| D4 | Noise level vs global noise models | PSDs use 1-h segments (3 h for L?? channels) with 50 % overlap. Each segment is split into 13 sub-segments with 75 % overlap, smoothed in full octaves at 1/8-octave steps, and binned in 1-dB bins to form a PDF. `pct_below_nlnm` and `pct_above_nhnm` are averaged only for f < Nyquist/1.5, to avoid the anti-alias roll-off. Wrong gain, wrong units or a missing response shifts the whole PDF by a constant number of dB relative to the NLNM/NHNM and to neighbouring stations. | Any time below the NLNM is suspect, since the NLNM is a global minimum. Gross unit errors give large constant offsets: a factor 10^3 in amplitude is 60 dB in power, and 10^9 is 180 dB (this arithmetic is ours). | Peterson 1993 [S: scanned PDF, no text layer; used through the MUSTANG implementation]; McNamara & Buland 2004 [A]; `PSDMetric.R` [F] | MISSING |
| D5 | Dead or disconnected channel | `dead_channel_gsn` flags a channel when the median PDF over 4–8 s period lies more than 5 dB below the NLNM on average (for sample rates of at least 1 sps). `dead_channel_lin` is the standard deviation of residuals from a straight-line fit of the mean PSD against log10(period): a flat digitizer-noise spectrum gives a small value. A dead channel still "has data", so availability checks pass. | 5 dB below NLNM at 4–8 s | `PSDMetric.R` [F] | MISSING (stations_with_data counts a dead channel as data) |
| D6 | Spikes and glitches | `num_spikes` runs a Hampel (rolling-median MAD) filter with a 41-sample window and a threshold of 10 MAD, and counts runs of outliers. NMSOP: glitches (spikes, steps from gaps or mass re-centring, clipped amplitudes, acausal FIR precursors) create false picks and, once filtered, cannot be told apart from real signals by most pickers. | Window 41 samples, threshold 10 | `spikesMetric.R` [F]; NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.6.3 [F] | MISSING |
| D7 | DC offsets and mass re-centring | `dc_offset` uses 1800-s windows stepped by 900 s and flags a jump where abs(Δmean) / mean(sd) > 0.9. | Ratio 0.9 | `DCOffsetTimesMetric.R` [F] | MISSING |
| D8 | Clipping and saturation | miniSEED SOH flag counts (`digitizer_clipping`, `amplifier_saturation`) and `max_range` in 300-s windows. NMSOP: clipped records block reliable S detection and must be removed before automatic S picking. | Flag counts above 0 | `stateOfHealthMetric.R`, `maxRangeMetric.R` [F]; NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 [F]; Diehl et al. 2009b [S via NMSOP] | MISSING (the energetic windows may show clipping, but nothing tests for it) |
| D9 | Clock quality flags | miniSEED `timing_quality` (blockette 1001), `clock_locked`, `suspect_time_tag` and `timing_correction`. Ringler et al. (2021): GSN timing meets its 10 ms design goal unless blockette 1001 shows a compromised clock. Yang et al. (2022): most of 5131 timing-error cases were tagged with low timing quality, "but there are quite a few exceptions". | 10 ms (GSN design goal); errors found from tens of ms to more than 10 s | `stateOfHealthMetric.R` [F]; Ringler et al. 2021 [A]; Yang, Song & Ringler 2022 [A] | MISSING (and flags are often absent in assembled archives) |
| D10 | Relative clock error from ambient-noise cross-correlation | Time-symmetry analysis. Clock drift shifts the causal and acausal surface-wave arrivals in opposite directions (an even term in δτ(t) and δτ(−t)). A change in the medium shifts both the same way (an odd term). Noise-source changes are reduced by longer stacks and checked by agreement across two period bands (5–10 s and 10–20 s) and by closure over station triplets. Clock errors of 0.5 s were found in long-running Californian stations, with a closure residual below 0.02 s (Stehly). Combining several component pairs reaches about 20 ms (1σ) and found jumps of up to 6 min after GPS loss, plus about 1-s jumps at three OBSs that probably came from missing samples (Hable). The method needs a station with a correct clock to fix absolute time, and uneven noise illumination biases the result. Drift resolution is about 0.1 s/yr (Naranjo). | About 10–20 ms precision with multi-component stacks; windows of 1 day to 1 month | Stehly et al. 2007 [F]; Hable et al. 2018 [F-part]; Naranjo et al. 2024 [F-part]; Sens-Schönfelder 2008 [S] | MISSING |
| D11 | Timing from earthquake data (station-term time series) | Relative arrival times from cross-correlated similar-event clusters give station terms in 3-month bins. Nine of the SCSN stations showed abrupt 20–70 ms offsets. P and S offsets were equal, which points to an instrumental cause, and five of them coincided with documented equipment changes (VCO and filter swaps). Repeating M5–5.9 events give a global timing check with about 10 ms precision (Yang). | Detects steps of 10–20 ms or more with CC differential times | Lin, Shearer & Hauksson 2008 [F-part]; Yang et al. 2022 [A] | MISSING (station residuals are summarised overall, not as a time series) |
| D12 | Horizontal orientation | `orientation_check` uses M ≥ 7 events, a window from 20 s before to 600 s after the Rayleigh arrival (distance / 4 km/s), and a 0.02–0.04 Hz bandpass. It scans azimuths and maximises the correlation of R with the Hilbert-transformed Z. This needs teleseismic surface waves, which short local windows rarely contain. | Reports azimuth error in degrees and requires Czr to be high | ISPAQ `orientationCheck_metrics.py` [F]; Ekström & Busby 2008 [A] | MISSING (low priority for P/S picking and location) |
| D13 | Polarity | `polarity_check` cross-correlates each station with its nearest neighbour for M ≥ 6.5 events (600-s window, maximum lag 10 s). Metadata with dip > 0 on Z means inverted polarity. Large-scale screening uses surface-wave relative-time anomalies followed by a P-arrival check (Zhao), and agreement of first motions with mechanisms (Lentas). | A negative correlation peak with a neighbour | ISPAQ `crossCorrelation_metrics.py` [F]; Zhao et al. 2025 [F-part, English abstract]; Lentas 2021 [S] | MISSING (matters for CC differential times and first-motion mechanisms) |
| D14 | Gain and response correctness | `transfer_function` computes the cross-spectrum of co-located sensors at 5–7 s and compares the data gain ratio and phase difference with what the metadata predict. GSN target accuracy is 1 % in amplitude and 1° in phase, checked with normal modes and tides. A single-event analysis cannot resolve gain errors much below about 20 %. Example of a gain error: the Wood–Anderson gain of 2800 vs 2080 produced a 0.13 step in catalog ML. | 1 %, 1°; about 20 % detectability from one event | `transferFunctionMetric.R` [F]; Davis, Ishii & Masters 2005 [S via IDA page]; LDEO WQC report [S]; Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part] | MISSING (the magnitude-stage note covers station ML terms) |
| D15 | Filter phase and processing time shift | Filters must be zero-phase, and reference and automatic picking must use the same filters. Pick times can differ by several seconds between frequency bands. Acausal FIR anti-alias filters create precursory ringing before impulsive onsets. | No number given beyond "zero-phase"; compare with the 10 ms timing goal and ML pick MAE of about 0.06 s | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.6.2 [F] | processing_time_shift (0.1 s; probably too loose, see §3) |
| P1 | Pick accuracy against consistent reference picks | Calibrate the picker on half of a consistently repicked reference set and test on the other half. Routine network picks are too noisy to serve as reference. Reference sets must flag seismograms with no identifiable phase, so that rejection by the picker can be tested. | PhaseNet counts a true positive at Δt < 0.1 s and reports μ and σ for Δt < 0.5 s. Münchmeyer counts outliers at > 0.45 s (regional) or > 1.5 s (teleseismic). In-domain P MAE is 0.08 s (STEAD), 0.12 (ETHZ), 0.14 (SCEDC), 0.21 (Iquique), 0.26 (INSTANCE), 0.57–0.74 s teleseismic. S MAE is 25–60 % worse than P. EQT vs JMA manual picks: σ ≈ 0.08 s, MAE ≈ 0.06 s, mean 0.01 s. | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.6.1 [F]; Zhu & Beroza [F-part]; Münchmeyer et al. 2022 [F]; Mousavi et al. 2020 [F-part] | pick_onset_offset (a reference-free proxy) |
| P2 | Baseline uncertainty of human picks | Manual P error is about 0.1 s at high SNR for local and regional events, and analysts are biased late. Agencies differ by 0.2–0.6 s (1σ across institutions) while each picks consistently on its own. Explosions have about 0.1 s error and teleseismic M4.6–6.1 events about 0.5 s. SCSN weights: 0 means within one sample, 1 means 2–3 samples. | 0.1 s; 0.2–0.6 s between agencies | Zeiler & Velasco 2009 and Douglas et al. 1997 [S via NMSOP Ch. 16]; Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part] | Sets the floor for any pick-accuracy threshold |
| P3 | Systematic offset of automatic picks vs onset | Baer–Kradolfer picks late (it cannot pick before the energetic onset), Allen picks early, and AIC quality is unreliable when based on SNR alone (Ch. 16). ML labels are Gaussians (σ = 0.1 s) centred on analyst picks, so ML picks reproduce the labelling convention. In-domain residuals are Laplacian with mode 0. Cross-domain picks are shifted: models trained on NEIC pick early on GEOFON data and vice versa ("agencies exercise different judgement"). One INSTANCE label was about 0.5 s late, and the models picked the true onset. | Median should be about 0 in-domain; offsets of several tenths of a second appear across agencies or domains | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 [F]; Münchmeyer et al. 2022 [F]; Zhu & Beroza [F-part] | pick_onset_offset (P only; S not judged) |
| P4 | Quality classes and weights | Discrete classes 0–4 (weights 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0) as in HYPOINVERSE and VELEST. Each class should map to a measured uncertainty interval in seconds. Calibration test: very few low-quality reference picks may be upgraded to the top class, and for high-precision work none. SNR alone is insufficient because noise bursts give high SNR. Signal duration should be required too, e.g. at least 70 % of a 4-s envelope above 2 × noise. ML probability correlates only weakly with pick accuracy. EQT's epistemic uncertainty did not track pick error. | 4 s envelope, 70 %, 2 × noise (example values) | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.5.1 [F]; Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part]; Pita-Sllim et al. 2023 [A]; Mousavi et al. 2020 [F-part] | pick_snr (report-only); weight calibration MISSING |
| P5 | Phase identification | Pickers can miss the first arrival and pick a later or larger phase: Pn missed and PmP picked, with errors of several seconds. Pick uncertainty has two parts, timing and phase identity. Reject S picks mistaken for P by comparing energy on the vertical and horizontal components. Check the consistency of an event's P picks with a jackknife. | Errors of several seconds near crossover distances | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.5.2, §16.3.3 [F]; Diehl et al. 2009a [A] | MISSING |
| P6 | Wadati diagram | Plot S−P against P arrival time per event, or ts against tp. The points should be linear with slope Vp/Vs − 1. Outliers identify mis-picked S independently of the velocity model. A robust (Huber) fit is needed because outliers bias least squares. | Slope about 0.73 for Vp/Vs 1.73; flag points that deviate from the robust line | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.5.3 [F]; Kisslinger & Engdahl 1973 [S]; Lin & Shearer 2007 [F-part] | MISSING |
| P7 | Location-residual outliers | Large residuals flag bad picks. Residual-based reweighting depends on the velocity model, so it must never replace picker weights, or correct picks will be down-weighted where the model is poor. Robust EDT locators tolerate outliers. Large-residual automatic picks are flagged for analyst review. | No fixed number. Example: an OBS study kept events with rms < 0.6 s and at least 8 phases. | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.5.3 [F]; Liu & Tan [F-part] | Station residual outliers and azimuthal pattern (report-only) |
| P8 | Picks on noise | Indicators include the fraction of picks that are associated, diurnal modulation and picks at cultural sources. Only 11.9–18.6 % of ML picks were associated at the Blanco OBS deployment, and the catalogs were still publishable. At Durrës the number of associated events per hour rose during quiet night hours and the smallest events were detected then, which the authors read as true events; cultural false picks would peak in daytime. EQT produced false positives on impulsive cultural noise in Texas. PhaseNet: requiring both P and S reduces false detections. | Association fraction of 12–19 % even for good OBS work | Liu & Tan [F-part]; Woollam et al. [F-part]; Mousavi et al. 2020 [F-part]; Zhu & Beroza [F-part] | association stage (associated_share at least 0.2, busy hours); diurnal test MISSING |
| P9 | S/P count | Few S picks means S was lost in the P coda, on clipped records, at vertical-only stations, or under a high picker threshold. Many S picks means picks on noise or water reverberation. ML yields 2–3 × more picks than STA/LTA at SCSN, especially S. OBS ML pickers gave S/P of about 1.65–1.8 (PickBlue 4.71 M / 2.85 M; OBSTransformer 9.43 M / 5.29 M) and EQT about 1.0. | No published bound found; observed 0.3–1.8 | Liu & Tan [F-part]; SCSN abstract [S]; Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part] | s_to_p_ratio (lower bound only) |
| P10 | Duplicate and multiple picks | Overlapping prediction windows (EQT overlap trades consistency against speed; 55 s recommended) and multiple peaks per trace (GPD needs a very high threshold). Co-located channels picked twice. | 55 s overlap; threshold 0.1 (EQT on NZ data) | Pita-Sllim et al. 2023 [A]; Münchmeyer et al. 2022 [F] | duplicate_picks |
| P11 | Pick completeness after mainshocks | Analysts and pickers both struggle with overlapping aftershocks. A rising long-term average desensitised STA/LTA triggering for days to months after Kern County, Landers and Hector Mine. Residual scatter of more than 20 ms increased for about 3 months near the Landers and Northridge ruptures. Multi-event scenarios are under-represented in ML benchmarks. | Days to months | Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part]; Lin et al. 2008 [F-part]; EQMamba EGU26 [S] | silent_stations and station_day_gaps (only partial); pick rate vs time since mainshock MISSING |
| P12 | Domain shift | Regional ↔ teleseismic transfer fails. Region-to-region transfer loses a little, but up to 10 % of picks become outliers (> 0.45 s). Land models degrade on OBS data, and OBS-trained PickBlue reaches 0.05 s P and 0.12 s S MAD. Different pickers on the same OBS data gave very different depth distributions (average vertical errors of 62, 115 and 381 m) and off-fault seismicity. | Up to 10 % outliers cross-domain | Münchmeyer et al. 2022 [F]; Bornstein et al. [F-part]; Liu & Tan [F-part] | Indirect only (pick_onset_offset, residuals) |
| P13 | Network practice | SCSN: automatic locations are reviewed by an analyst during the working day or on demand. Since 1977 all digital phase data were reviewed and repicked where needed (TIMIT, Jiggle). Weight definitions drifted with technology. Temporary digital stations deployed for Landers and Northridge had incorrect clocks. Before telemetry, each station had its own clock, which affected absolute P times but not S−P. A common telemetry clock removes clock error from locations. ISC: picks from different agencies are inconsistent. NMSOP: regional tomography needs complete repicking. | Not applicable | Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part]; NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 [F]; Diehl et al. 2009a [A] | Not applicable (context) |

## 2. Attribution: from anomaly to cause

| Anomaly | Candidate causes | Discriminating evidence or test | Attributable? Confounds | Source (read level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A constant offset in one station's P residual over the whole deployment | (a) Constant clock offset. (b) Error in station elevation or coordinates. (c) Slow or fast near-surface structure (site delay). (d) Instrument phase response (a different filter chain). (e) Pick convention at that station, e.g. a different channel type. | Compare the S offset with the P offset. A clock or instrument error gives δS = δP. Structure gives δS ≈ (Vp/Vs)·δP, about 1.7 × δP for a near-surface delay with a similar ray path (our inference from Lin 2008's argument). A coordinate error gives an azimuth-dependent residual (first harmonic with back-azimuth) rather than a constant. Relative clock against neighbours: an even (symmetric) noise-CC shift that is the same in two period bands. A shift that depends on frequency points to instrument phase response. | Partly. δS/δP separates clock from structure only when S picks are plentiful and S site effects do not differ much from P. A constant clock offset and a site delay with δS ≈ δP cannot be told apart from earthquake residuals, because the station term absorbs both. Only an external time reference or noise CC with a well-timed neighbour separates them. A common-mode offset at every station trades against origin time and cannot be seen in residuals at all. | Lin et al. 2008 [F-part]; Stehly et al. 2007 [F]; Naranjo et al. 2024 [F-part] |
| A step in a station's residual time series | (a) Clock jump after GPS loss. (b) Equipment swap with a different phase response. (c) Missing or duplicated samples. (d) Station moved without a metadata update. (e) Real velocity change, e.g. after a mainshock. | A step coincides with an equipment-change log, a metadata epoch or SOH flags (clock_locked, timing_quality). Equal P and S steps point to an instrument. Steps of exactly one sample period point to sample slips. Noise-CC drift shows a jump in the even part. A medium change gives δS ≠ δP and spatial coherence across stations near the rupture. | Mostly yes when cross-correlation differential times or many events are available. Lin et al. traced 5 of 9 offsets to documented swaps; 3 had no documented change, and one offset (1990) preceded the documented change (1995). A clock jump and a phase-response change both give δS = δP; only the change log or noise CC across frequency bands separates them. Stehly notes that the even term D covers "clock drift (or a phase shift of the sensor response)". | Lin et al. 2008 [F-part]; Hable et al. 2018 [F-part]; Stehly et al. 2007 [F] |
| A gradual drift in residuals or noise-CC lag | (a) Clock drift: linear on OBSs, non-linear on land stations after GPS loss. (b) Seasonal change in noise sources. (c) Real velocity change. | Even vs odd symmetry of the noise CC. Agreement in two period bands, since noise sources differ between bands. Closure over triplets. A weighted inversion by inter-station distance reduces bias from illumination. Seasonal (annual) periodicity points to the noise sources. | Yes for relative drift between stations, at about 10–20 ms or 0.1 s/yr. Absolute time is meaningless without at least one station with a correct clock. Uneven illumination mimics drift. Stehly saw about 0.5 s annual fluctuations from source seasonality and needed 6-month windows. | Stehly et al. 2007 [F]; Naranjo et al. 2024 [F-part]; Hable et al. 2018 [F-part] |
| PSD far below the NLNM or flat | (a) Dead or disconnected sensor (digitizer noise only). (b) Gain too small, or wrong units such as counts treated as m/s. (c) Wrong response, e.g. an accelerometer response on a velocity channel. | The spectral shape: digitizer noise is flat, with a small dead_channel_lin. A constant dB offset at all frequencies relative to neighbours and the NLNM points to gain or units. An offset that depends on frequency and follows the response corners points to poles and zeros. | Yes for gross errors (tens of dB). Dead vs very quiet is ambiguous at short periods. Use the 4–8 s microseism band, which should always be above the NLNM. | `PSDMetric.R` [F]; McNamara & Buland 2004 [A] |
| PSD far above the NHNM | (a) Gain too large or unit error (e.g. nm treated as m). (b) Cultural or wind noise, which is real. (c) Failing sensor (long-period noise, mass off-centre). (d) Spikes or glitches. | A real noise source shows a diurnal or weekly cycle and is confined to its frequency band. A gain error is a constant offset across bands. A failing sensor raises the long-period end and coincides with DC offsets and re-centring steps. Spike counts. | A gross gain error vs a real noisy site is mostly separable by the constant offset and the absence of a diurnal cycle. A moderate gain error (below about 20 %) vs site amplification is fundamentally ambiguous without a co-located sensor or a calibration. | `PSDMetric.R`, `spikesMetric.R`, `DCOffsetTimesMetric.R` [F]; WQC report [S] |
| Constant station magnitude residual | (a) Gain or sensitivity error. (b) Unit error (mm vs nm). (c) Wrong response. (d) Site amplification. (e) Error in the attenuation term. | Units give round-number offsets (log10 of 10^3 = 3, or 6). Gain errors coincide with a PSD offset relative to neighbours. Site amplification is frequency-dependent and real; it also raises the noise PSD but not the long-period microseism level equally. A step in time points to a metadata epoch. | Units: yes. Gain vs site: no, without an independent calibration (co-located sensors, tides or modes). Example: the WA gain of 2800 vs 2080 produced a 0.13 magnitude step that was found only by instrument analysis. | Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part]; Davis et al. 2005 [S] |
| Waveform anti-correlated with neighbours | (a) Polarity reversal from cable, digitizer sign or metadata dip sign. (b) True radiation pattern for near-source P first motions. (c) Clock error by half a period, for surface-wave phase measurements. | Long-period or teleseismic coherence with the nearest station, where radiation is the same. The metadata dip and sensitivity sign. Zhao et al.: clock errors and polarity reversals both appear as surface-wave phase anomalies, and a second check of P arrival times separates them, since a clock error moves P and a reversal does not. | Yes with long-period or teleseismic data. With local events only, a reversal vs a nodal first motion needs many events (a consistent sign across mechanisms). Lentas found that flagged stations mix questionable picking with true reversals, so a waveform check is needed. | ISPAQ `crossCorrelation_metrics.py` [F]; Zhao et al. 2025 [F-part]; Lentas 2021 [S] |
| Many picks not associated (picks stage) | (a) Picks on cultural, OBS-reverberation or instrumental noise. (b) Station metadata error, so the picks cannot fit. (c) Clock error, same effect. (d) Real small events below the association threshold. (e) Domain shift producing low-confidence picks. | Check whether unassociated picks concentrate at a few stations or are network-wide. A daytime peak points to cultural noise. Real events cluster in time across stations and are relatively more common at night. Check the residual of associated picks at the same station: if large, suspect metadata or clock. | Partly. Cultural vs microseismic is attributable with a diurnal test. Small real events vs noise needs waveform review or template matching. A low association fraction alone is not diagnostic: 12–19 % in a published OBS study. | Woollam et al. [F-part]; Liu & Tan [F-part]; Mousavi et al. 2020 [F-part] |
| S/P ratio too low | (a) Vertical-only stations. (b) Clipped records. (c) S buried in P coda at short distances. (d) Picker S threshold too high, or a P-only model. (e) Overlapping aftershocks. | Compute S/P per station and compare with each station's component set. Check clipping flags. Check S/P against distance (short distances lose S in the coda). Check S/P against time after the mainshock. | Mostly attributable per station using metadata and simple waveform statistics. | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.4 [F]; Hutton et al. 2010 [F-part] |
| S/P ratio too high, or S picks with large residuals | (a) S picked on noise or reverberation. (b) P coda converted phases picked as S. (c) P picked as S. | Wadati outliers per event. S−P vs distance consistency. NMSOP energy ratio between vertical and horizontal components. | Attributable with a Wadati diagram, which does not depend on the velocity model. | NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 §16.5.3 [F] |
| Systematic offset of picks vs AIC onset | (a) Picker labelling convention (agency bias). (b) Causal filter phase shift in preprocessing. (c) Low-frequency or emergent onsets, where AIC itself is biased. (d) Domain shift. | Compare with processing_time_shift on the same records. A processing shift is constant per filter and similar at all stations. Picker bias grows with emergent onsets, low SNR and low dominant frequency. Compare P and S. | Partly. The AIC reference has its own bias, so only large or station-dependent offsets are meaningful. A clock error cannot appear here, because the AIC onset and the pick come from the same record. | Münchmeyer et al. 2022 [F]; NMSOP-2 Ch. 16 [F] |
| Silent station or station-day gaps | (a) Data gap. (b) Dead channel. (c) The agent picked the wrong channel or location code. (d) Metadata missing, so the picker skipped the station. (e) Very high noise. | Data availability (D1). PSD (D4 and D5). Whether the station has picks on other days. Which channel codes were read. | Yes for (a)–(d) with the data-stage metrics. (e) is real and not an error. | `PSDMetric.R` [F] |
| Duplicate picks | (a) Overlapping windows not merged. (b) Co-located channels (HH and EH, or two location codes) picked separately. (c) Several probability peaks. (d) Real overlapping events. | The channel and location of each member of a duplicate pair. A peaked distribution of separations points to a window-overlap artifact. A broad distribution with association to two events points to real events. | Yes from the pick table alone. | Münchmeyer et al. 2022 [F]; Pita-Sllim et al. 2023 [A] |
| Large residuals at a particular distance range | (a) Phase misidentification at the Pg/Pn crossover (PmP picked). (b) Real lateral structure. | Residual vs distance shows a step at the crossover. ## 3. Key insights for an automated checker

**Reliable automatically, with numeric rules already in operational use.** These checks need no reference catalog and no velocity model:
- Availability, gaps and sample-rate agreement (1 % with metadata, 15 % with the response).
- Spikes (Hampel filter, 41 samples, 10 MAD) and DC offsets (0.9 ratio).
- Dead channels (median 4–8 s more than 5 dB below the NLNM).
- Gross gain and unit errors as constant dB offsets against the NLNM/NHNM and against neighbouring stations.
- SOH flags, when present.
- Duplicate picks.
- Per-station S/P.
- Wadati linearity per event.

All of these are deterministic, and their causes are named directly by the metric.

**The P-vs-S ratio is the most useful reference-free attribution tool for timing.** Lin et al. (2008) separated instrumental from structural station-term changes only because P and S shifted equally. Our report-only station residual check should therefore report δP, δS and their ratio per station, and residuals binned in time (a step detector). Today it reports a single residual summary.

**Some timing errors cannot be seen from inside the catalog.**
- A timing error common to all stations is absorbed by origin time.
- A constant per-station clock offset over a short deployment is absorbed by the station term, together with elevation and site delay.
- pick_onset_offset cannot see any timing error, because the AIC onset and the pick come from the same record.

Only SOH flags, GPS logs, or noise CC against a well-timed station can expose these errors. Noise CC with a well-timed station is feasible for networks with days to weeks of data, at about 10–20 ms precision with several components (Hable). Without such evidence the checker should say "timing not verifiable", not "timing OK".

**Our thresholds compared with the literature.**
- processing_time_shift at 0.1 s is ten times the GSN timing goal (10 ms) and larger than in-domain ML pick MAE (0.06–0.14 s). A tolerance of 1–2 samples (about 0.01–0.02 s at 100 sps) would match how weight 0 is defined (one sample). This is our recommendation.
- pick_onset_offset at 0.2 s on the median is loose. In-domain residuals have mode 0, so a median offset above about 0.05 s already signals a convention or filter problem. The fraction of picks above 0.45 s (Münchmeyer's regional outlier level) is a better per-pick measure. S should be judged too, with a larger tolerance (S MAE is 25–60 % worse than P).
- The s_to_p_ratio lower bound of 0.3 is consistent with the literature, but an upper bound is missing. ML pickers on OBS data reach S/P of about 1.7, and S/P well above 1 at land stations is suspicious. This is our inference; we found no published bound.
- associated_share at least 0.2 (association stage) would have flagged a published OBS study with 12–19 % association. Treat it as REPORT_ONLY for OBS or noisy networks.

**What still needs an analyst.** We found no automated method that resolves these reliably:
- Phase identity near crossover distances (Pn/PmP/Pg).
- Emergent onsets.
- Whether a station residual reflects real structure or an error when δS/δP ≈ 1 and the offset is constant.
- Nodal first motion vs reversal with local events only.
- Whether unassociated picks are real micro-events or noise.

NMSOP explicitly leaves flagged large-residual picks for review by the network analyst. ML probability is a weak proxy for pick accuracy (Pita-Sllim [A]; EQT), so agent-supplied probabilities cannot replace picker quality classes.

**Fundamentally ambiguous.** No check removes these ambiguities:
- A constant clock offset vs site delay (when δS ≈ δP).
- A gain error below about 20 % vs site amplification at a single-sensor station.
- Absolute time on OBS or isolated stations with no reference.
- A common-mode timing error vs origin time.

The checker should report these as "unverifiable" and name the extra evidence that would resolve them: a GPS log, a co-located sensor, or a noise-CC neighbour.

**MISSING checks, in priority order.** All are reference-free.
1. Wadati diagram per event: robust slope and outlier S picks. Also per-station S−P consistency.
2. Station residual decomposition: δP, δS, δS/δP, time-binned steps, azimuth harmonic.
3. PSD-PDF vs NLNM/NHNM and vs the network median after the agent's response removal: dead channel, unit and gain errors.
4. Clipping and saturation detection near energetic windows. Flag picks on clipped records.
5. Spike, DC-offset and glitch scan, since these are sources of false picks.
6. Diurnal modulation of total and unassociated pick rates.
7. Pick rate vs time since mainshock, to catch picks missed in the coda.
8. An upper bound on S/P, and S/P per station.
9. SOH timing flags, when present.
10. sample_rate_resp, when the agent supplies a response.
11. Optional noise-CC relative clock check for OBS or temporary networks.
12. Polarity consistency between neighbours, because CC differential times depend on sign.
13. Pick-weight calibration: the residual should grow monotonically as weight or probability falls.
