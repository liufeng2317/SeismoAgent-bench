# Magnitude and catalog-level QC: correctness criteria and anomaly attribution

Scope: how seismologists judge whether magnitudes are correct and whether a catalog as a whole is complete, homogeneous and free of artefacts, and whether an obvious anomaly can be attributed to a cause. Covers local-magnitude QC (network averaging, station residuals and corrections, distance trends, saturation and clipping, amplitude conventions, ML vs Mw/Md), completeness (MAXC, GFT, MBS, EMR, Mc(t), Mc maps), b-value pitfalls, short-term aftershock incompleteness, temporal homogeneity (rate changes and magnitude shifts), quarry blasts, duplicates and inter-agency comparison. Magnitude formulas are not re-reviewed (the project already holds NMSOP-2 IS 3.3 / DS 3.1, IASPEI 2013, Hutton & Boore 1987, Uhrhammer 2011, GB 17740-2017).

## 1. Correctness criteria

Our checks: magnitude_range, b_value (report-only), magnitude_detection, magnitude_recompute, largest_events_magnitude, rate_after_largest (report-only), and duplicate_events (location stage, 1.5 s / 3 km). "MISSING" = an expert criterion with no counterpart in the checker.

| # | Criterion | Method | Typical thresholds / numbers | Source | Our check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Robust network magnitude | Event ML = median (or trimmed mean) of channel MLs; each horizontal component is a separate datum, no vector sum; outliers removed | SCSN: median of channels, Chauvenet outlier threshold 0.5, ML preferred only with > 5 calibrated channels. SeisComP default: mean if < 4 station magnitudes, else trimmed mean; "median trimmed mean" drops values > 0.5 from the median; ≥ 4 stations recommended for automatic systems | Hutton et al. 2010; IASPEI standard in ISC Bulletin Summary; SeisComP scmag docs | magnitude_recompute uses the median of its own station MLs; the agent's own per-event station count and spread are not checked → MISSING (per-event station-ML count and spread) |

Mca vs ML scatter ≈ 0.3 | Hutton & Boore 1987 (in project); Di Bona 2016 [abs]; Hutton et al. 2010 | MISSING. | M8 | ML–Mw scaling consistency | Compare ML with Mw for a subset; expect ML ∝ 1.5 Mw below Mw ~2–4 (constant corner frequency), ≈ 1:1 above; geographic Mw−ML offsets up to ±0.3 are physical | NC: mean Mw−ML = −0.06 (N = 397) but +0.26 Geysers, +0.19 Cape Mendocino, −0.28 eastern California; ML ∝ 1.5 Mw below ~Mw 2–4 [abs] | Gee et al. 2004; Deichmann 2017 [abs]; Munafò et al. 2016 [abs]; Herrmann & Marzocchi 2021 | Not a checker criterion (no Mw in agent runs); needed for attribution of b-values (§2 D) |
| M9 | Coda/duration magnitude QC | Calibrate Md/Mca against good ML by regression; flag Md inconsistent with number of picks or with WA availability; check ML−Md by region | Mca ≈ ML from 1.0 to 3.5 with scatter ≈ 0.3, overestimates in the upper part; a 1981 Mca calibration error corrected by −0.4; Md biased high in sequences when later events fall in the coda; ML−Mc: tectonic +0.048, explosions −0.137 to −0.196 (Utah) [abs] | Hutton et al. 2010; Woessner et al. 2010; Koper et al. 2016 [abs] | Only relevant if an agent uses Md. The generalisation, "magnitude consistent with how many / how far stations detected the event", is magnitude_detection (aggregate only); per-event outliers MISSING |

| C1 | Completeness magnitude (bulk) | MAXC (mode of non-cumulative FMD) + 0.2; GFT (R = 90 or 95 %, fallback MAXC); MBS (first cutoff with |b_ave − b| ≤ δb_Shi-Bolt, b_ave over 0.5 units); EMR (G-R above Mc + normal CDF below); bootstrap 200 samples for σ | MAXC and GFT-95 % underestimate Mc by ≈ 0.1 on synthetics; MBS highest; n_min ≈ 200 (MAXC stable at much smaller n); MBS-WW best overall, needs ≈ 300 events above Mc; examples: NCSN MAXC 1.20, GFT 1.12, MBS 1.48, EMR 1.29 | Mignan & Woessner 2012; Woessner & Wiemer 2005 [via M&W]; Wang et al. 2025 | b_value uses MAXC + 0.2 (as Herrmann & Marzocchi). MISSING: second method (MBS or Lilliefors) to expose curved/inconsistent FMDs |
| C2 | Completeness in time Mc(t) | Moving window of events (e.g. 1000 events, step 250; or 500 per sample), MAXC/MBASS with bootstrap; rate-based Mc(t) for sequences | Landers: Mc up to two units higher in the first days; networks: Mc steps of 0.1–0.3 at upgrades (Italy 1.9 → 1.7 → 1.6) | Mignan & Woessner 2012; Gulia et al. 2012; Hainzl 2016 | MISSING (Mc(t) series); rate_after_largest is a coarse proxy |
| C3 | Short-term aftershock incompleteness (STAI) | Expected Mc after a mainshock: Mc(M, t) = M − 4.5 − 0.75 log10 t (t in days, Mc ≥ 2; southern California, M ≥ 6); rate-based limit r_max ≈ 100–200 events/day above Mc0, blind time Δt ≈ 7–80 s | M 7.1: Mc ≈ 4.2 at 10 min, ≈ 3.6 at 1 h, ≈ 2.6 at 1 day (derived from the formula); Kagan 2004: up to ≈ 28,000 Landers aftershocks missing (≈ two thirds of M 2 events) [via H&M]; Hainzl: STAI lasts far longer than coda (M 2 incomplete 4.6 days after M 7, coda ≈ 1000 s) | Helmstetter et al. 2006 [via Hainzl]; Hainzl 2016; Kagan 2004 [abs] | rate_after_largest (report-only) looks for a rate drop; MISSING: "events above Mc(M, t) present in the coda of the largest events" (the known missed-M4+ failure) |
| C4 | Spatial completeness | Mc map (constant N or R per node; Nmin 50, ≥ 20 events above Mc); BMC prior Mc = c1 d^c2 + c3 with d = distance to the k-th (k ≈ 4) nearest station; PMC from station detection probabilities | Italy: onshore 1.6–1.9, offshore 2.6, deep 3.5; Taiwan prior c1 = 5.96, c2 = 0.0803, c3 = −5.80, σ = 0.18 (k = 4, km); PMC Mp can be 1 unit lower than catalog Mc | Gulia et al. 2012; Mignan & Woessner 2012; Mignan et al. 2011 [via M&W] | magnitude_detection is a related, magnitude-scale check. MISSING: small magnitudes far outside the network (events smaller than any network could detect there) |
| B1 | b-value estimator | Aki–Utsu ML with binning correction b = log10 e / (⟨M⟩ − (Mc − ΔM/2)); or Tinti–Mulargia; uncertainty Shi & Bolt δb = 2.3 b² sqrt(Σ(Mi−⟨M⟩)²/(N(N−1))); least squares on the FMD is biased | Continuous-magnitude formula without −ΔM/2 is strongly biased and underestimates σ even at ΔM = 0.1; Shi–Bolt unbiased with the corrected estimator; magnitude errors add negligible bias; b ≈ 1 (0.9–1.2 in California); estimate only with N ≥ 100 | Marzocchi & Sandri 2003; Marzocchi et al. 2020 [abs]; Herrmann & Marzocchi 2021; Hainzl 2016 | b_value applies the binning correction and needs ≥ 50 above Mc and range [0.6, 1.6]; literature asks N ≥ 100–200 and Shi–Bolt σ → minor gap |
| B2 | b-value robust to transient incompleteness | b-positive: use only positive magnitude differences between successive events (above a small δ); Laplace distribution with the same b | Classical estimator underestimates b when Mc rises after large events; b+ robust; loses efficiency if sub-threshold events are reported (b-more-incomplete fix) | van der Elst 2021 [abs]; Lippiello & Petrillo 2023 | MISSING (b+ alongside b_value; a large b+ − b gap diagnoses STAI) |

| D1 | Duplicates within a catalog | Pairs closer than a time/space window; in sequences use a normalised nearest-neighbour metric (DT, DX, DY in σ units, depth excluded) | ComCat automatic association: 16 s and 100 km [mailing list]; "1 min and ~100 km" windows fail in sequences (599 of 1175 first-day JMA events < 1 min from a neighbour); NN metric thresholds 5.3–5.9, errors 0.6–1.9 % | Vorobieva et al. 2022; USGS realtime-feed-users list 2016; NCEDC ANSS archive page | duplicate_events (1.5 s / 3 km, location stage) — consistent with sequence-safe practice |

## 2. Attribution: anomaly → causes → discriminating evidence

Reliability scale: high = one test separates the causes from the catalog plus waveforms; medium = needs an extra measurement we can make reference-free; low = confounded without external data (Mw, network logs, a reference catalog).

| Anomaly | Candidate causes | Discriminating evidence / test | Attributable? Confounds | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Constant magnitude offset, all sizes (catalog − recomputed) | (1) amplitude unit error: factors of 10³ (nm/µm) or 10⁶ (nm/mm) → 3 or 6 units, or partial when combined with a constant from another unit system (our run: mm amplitude with the nm constant, −2.68); (2) wrong response stage: velocity instead of displacement → log10(2πf) ≈ 0.8 at 1 Hz, 1.8 at 10 Hz (derived); one-zero WA applied to displacement (≈ −1.2, our run); (3) WA gain 2800 vs 2080 → 0.13; (4) amplitude definition (peak vs half peak-to-peak) → 0–0.3; (5) regional vs standard −logA0 and station corrections → 0.1–0.3 | Size of the offset (near an exact log10 unit factor → unit; 0.13 → gain; ≤ 0.3 → calibration); distance dependence of station residuals (flat → unit/gain/convention; sloped → −logA0); magnitude/frequency dependence (velocity–displacement errors scale with dominant frequency, so the offset changes with event size and distance); spread across stations (one or few stations → metadata of those stations) | High for offsets ≥ 0.5 with an exact unit or response signature; low for 0.1–0.3, where calibration, station terms and real regional attenuation are coupled ("calibration parameters are coupled together and to the magnitudes, a formidable inversion problem") | Woessner et al. 2010; Hutton et al. 2010; ISC/IASPEI; derived arithmetic |

## 3. Key insights for an automated checker

1. The literature's first rule for catalog statistics is "artefacts first": blasts, Mc heterogeneity and magnitude-scale changes must be found and removed before any b-value or rate interpretation; man-made rate changes reach ~40 % and look like physics (Gulia et al. 2012). For agent runs the analogue is checking homogeneity across the run's own internal switches (data gaps, picked vs template-matched events, different magnitude code paths) rather than across decades.
2. Offsets are attributable by their size and their dependence on distance, magnitude and station. A unit or response mistake leaves an exact signature (log10 of 10³, 10⁶, 2πf, or a run-specific constant such as −2.68 or −1.2) that is flat with distance; a calibration problem is ≤ 0.3, sloped with distance or region-specific. A station-residual table (per-station mean, per-distance-bin mean) is the single missing diagnostic that would let the checker say which (M2–M4).
3. Our 0.5 tolerance on magnitude_recompute is consistent with the literature: inter-agency ML differences of 0.1–0.2 (BK–CI), regional Mw−ML of ±0.3, a 0.13 WA-gain step and −0.07 from a statewide recalibration are all "normal". Offsets of 0.1–0.3 cannot be called errors reference-free.
4. MAXC + 0.2 is standard but is known to underestimate Mc in heterogeneous (bulk, sequence) catalogs; in high-resolution catalogs a goodness-of-fit Mc (Lilliefors) can be ~2 units higher, and no common Mc method detects mixed-type discontinuities. Keeping b_value report-only is supported; adding b-positive and a b-vs-cutoff profile would turn it into an attribution tool rather than a pass/fail.
5. Short-term incompleteness is expected, so a rate drop after the mainshock is not an error; but its size is bounded. Mc(M, t) = M − 4.5 − 0.75 log10 t (days) gives a conservative floor: an M 4+ event missing ten minutes after an M 7 is beyond normal STAI. This converts the known "missed M4+ in the coda" failure into a reference-free check (scan the largest events' codas on far unclipped stations for events above Mc(M, t)).
6. ML is not Mw: below Mw ~2–4 ML grows ~1.5× faster, so ML catalogs of small events have b ≈ 0.7–0.8 even when the Mw b ≈ 1; above M ~6 ML saturates. A low b from an ML catalog is not evidence of an error, and a high-M offset vs Mw is not either. The checker should not penalise these physics-driven differences, but should require magnitude-type labels and treat M ≥ 6 ML as needing Mw or a stated caveat.
7. Large events need their own gate (as our largest_events_magnitude does): SCSN practice excludes amplitudes "approaching the clipping level", uses SNR ≥ 8 and a magnitude-dependent distance cutoff (D = 170 ML − 205 km, ≥ 30 km). An SNR gate and the cutoff are missing from magnitude_recompute.
8. Day/night statistics give two cheap reference-free checks: a daytime excess (Rq ≥ 1.5, weekday pattern) flags blasts; the Rydelek–Sacks walkout (R > 1.73 √N at a given magnitude) flags incompleteness at that magnitude without assuming G-R (needs declustering; unstable on bulk data).
9. Duplicate detection with wide windows (16 s / 100 km, 1 min) is an inter-agency tool and misfires in sequences; our 1.5 s / 3 km window within one catalog is appropriate, but a pick-sharing test would make it attributable.
10. Comparisons with a reference (evaluation side) should use orthogonal regression and a sequence-safe matcher; least squares alone can fake a 0.3–0.4 conversion error and apparent incompleteness.
11. Attribution limits: (a) physical b-value change vs magnitude-scale compression vs incompleteness cannot be fully separated from the catalog alone; b+ and windowing remove incompleteness, but compression needs Mw on a subset; (b) a magnitude shift coinciding with an Mc change is non-unique; (c) calibration offsets ≤ 0.3 are coupled to station terms and attenuation. The checker should report these as "consistent with X or Y" rather than name one cause.
