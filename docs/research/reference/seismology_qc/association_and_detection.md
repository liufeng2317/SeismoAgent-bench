# Phase association and event detection QC: correctness criteria and anomaly attribution

Scope: how seismologists judge whether phase association (GaMMA, PyOcto, REAL, PhaseLink, GENIE, HEX-type and grid associators) and event detection (ML-picker catalogs, template matching / matched filtering, similarity search) are correct, and whether an obvious anomaly in the output can be attributed to a cause reliably. Covers split, merged, duplicate and false events, events with few picks, association in dense sequences, MAD thresholds, null tests, template quality, detections in mainshock coda, detections of distant events, template mislocation, detection magnitudes, completeness gain, temporal rate artefacts (network changes, data gaps, day/night cycles, cultural noise) and quarry-blast contamination, and how the large ML and template-matching catalogs validated themselves. Completeness statistics, b-value pitfalls and magnitude scales are covered in the sibling note `magnitude_and_catalog.md` and only cross-referenced here.

Date: 2026-10-31. Stage: association and detection of the reference-free checker (`docs/research/02_Checker_Design.md`, `seismo_checker/source/catalog_checker/checks.py`). Downloads and extracted text are in the session scratchpad (`lit_qc/association_detection`), not in the repository.

## 1. Correctness criteria

Our checks named in the last column: `associated_share`, `association_busy_hours`, `duplicate_events` (origins within 1.5 s and 3 km, flag above 2 %), `events_with_few_picks` (< 4 P picks, flag above 20 %), `event_travel_time_fit` (report-only), `rate_after_largest` (report-only), and the data-stage `energetic_windows`. MISSING means no current check covers the criterion; PLANNED means it is in the template-matching plan (detections per template, CC/MAD distribution, daily rate jumps, detection magnitudes).

### 1a. Association

| Criterion | What is checked and how | Typical thresholds / numbers | Source | Our check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share of picks associated | Fraction of input picks that end up in events; read together with the picker threshold | GaMMA, Ridgecrest 6 days, PhaseNet picks on 23 stations: 598,218 of 651,994 P (91.8 %) and 633,010 of 686,291 S (92.2 %) associated. PyOcto, Iquique, picker threshold deliberately 0.05: PyOcto and REAL associated 43–46 %, GaMMA 34 %; authors stress the remainder is not necessarily false (small events recorded on too few stations) | Zhu et al. 2022; Münchmeyer 2024 | `associated_share` (but no single correct value exists: it scales with the picker threshold; interpret per station and over time) |
| Minimum picks and stations per event | Event kept only with enough P, S and total picks, and enough stations with both P and S | REAL example `-S 3/2/5/2/0.5/...` (≥3 P, ≥2 S, ≥5 total, ≥2 stations with both P and S, origin-time spread std0 0.5 s); the guide says npsboth0 "can significantly improve your association reliability, especially when you have a poor station coverage". PyOcto benchmark and Iquique: ≥10 picks and ≥4 stations with P and S. Tan et al.: HypoDD only for events with ≥4 P and ≥7 total picks. PhaseLink: event precision > 0.996 for all n_min, recall falls 0.956 → 0.891 as n_min rises; n_min ≈ 8 sufficient. QTM: ≥12 template channels, i.e. ≥4 stations | REAL guide; Münchmeyer 2024; Tan et al. 2021; Ross et al. 2019 PhaseLink; Ross et al. 2019 QTM | `events_with_few_picks` (P only). MISSING: stations with both P and S (npsboth0-type) |
| Recovery of synthetic events (event- and pick-level precision/recall) | Associator run on synthetic arrivals plus random false picks (30 %, 100 %, 300 %); an output event matches a true event when ≥50 % (Puente et al., PhaseLink) or ≥60 % in both directions (PyOcto) of picks are shared | GENIE and PyOcto near-perfect for most scenarios, F1 > 0.8 even at highest noise and rate; PhaseLink near zero in the hardest subduction cases; GaMMA degrades at high noise and density. Pick-level: REAL (homogeneous model) misses ~3 picks per event, 5–10 with 1-D model; PyOcto and REAL absorb false picks at high event and noise rates | Puente et al. 2025; Münchmeyer 2024; Ross et al. 2019 PhaseLink | MISSING (a synthetic-injection test through the Agent's own associator would be reference-free) |
| Tolerance / velocity-model fit of associated picks | Residual distribution of associated picks against the tolerance window; clipping at the window edge | REAL guide: if the P and S residual distributions are "too narrow (clearly clipped), maybe your velocity model is too bad or the nrt is too small"; nrt usually 1–2. Too high a tolerance absorbs noise picks (lower precision), too low a tolerance drops true picks (lower recall) | REAL guide; Puente et al. 2025 §5.1 | `event_travel_time_fit` (report-only, counts outlier picks). MISSING: residual clipped at the tolerance edge |
| Pick-pattern consistency | A station far away should not pick when many closer stations are silent; events whose nearest station is far are suspicious | PyOcto: false events from distant stations "are easy to identify manually because of the inconsistent pick pattern, i.e., the existence of many non-picking stations between the picking stations"; `min_pick_fraction` keeps picks only out to the distance where the fraction of picking stations stays above a threshold. REAL `nxd`: discard events whose nearest station is farther than nxd × grid range | Münchmeyer 2024; REAL guide | MISSING (per event: share of closer stations without picks). Partly `outside_network` and `isolated_events` at the location stage |
| Duplicates and split events | Two origins for one earthquake (time-slice overlap, several templates, P-only and S-only halves) | PyOcto deduplicates after processing overlapping 20-min time slices. QTM merges detections < 2.0 s apart, keeps the highest stacked CC, and states that truly near-simultaneous events at different locations cannot be resolved. Neves et al. merge detections with overlapping windows on any channel, keep the highest CC. Puente et al. did not evaluate duplicates separately; in the subduction scenario GaMMA and PhaseLink turned missed picks into secondary false events ("more possibilities for a secondary event to achieve a consistent source location with several picks by chance"); requiring more picks per event reduced this. PhaseLink proposes back-projecting all nearby phases after location "to clean up or add in any split events" | Münchmeyer 2024; Ross et al. 2019 QTM supplement; Neves et al. 2023; Puente et al. 2025 §5.2; Ross et al. 2019 PhaseLink | `duplicate_events`. MISSING: split diagnostic (pairs close in time whose station sets are complementary or whose picks are one phase type only) |
| Capacity in dense sequences | Event rate and pick rate in the busiest periods; performance against mean inter-event time | PhaseLink synthetic stress test (Japan): precision 0.996 / recall 0.911 at 64 s mean spacing, 0.603 / 0.197 at 4 s; usable down to ~10–12 s; a grid associator (dbgrassoc) missed ~40 % even in the easiest case. Puente et al.: 2000 events per day on ~50 stations is "quite extreme". Iquique: GaMMA finds fewer events, gap largest on high-rate days; all associators miss events in the first days compared with Omori decay, and "our results can not distinguish if this is a limitation of the picking model or the association models". GaMMA, Ridgecrest: 32 events in an 8-min window where SCSN has 3 and the Ross et al. TM catalog 22 | Ross et al. 2019 PhaseLink; Puente et al. 2025; Münchmeyer 2024; Zhu et al. 2022 | `association_busy_hours` |
| Picks per event over time | Daily median P and S picks per event | Iquique: P picks per event correlate positively with daily event count; attributed to picker performance (more large impulsive events vs higher noise in high-rate periods) | Münchmeyer 2024 | MISSING (time series of picks per event) |
| Attrition between association and location | Associated events that fail location or relocation | EQTransformer, Tottori: ~15 % of associated events did not reach the final catalog; "hard to assign them as false detections". QTM: 38.7 % of detections relocated by GrowClust, the rest keep the template location | Mousavi et al. 2020; Ross et al. 2019 QTM supplement | MISSING |
| Associator location artefacts | Distinct location signatures per associator | GaMMA: shallow mislocations outside the network (surface initialisation); REAL: grid artefacts; PyOcto: depth stripes; "locations should only be treated as preliminary estimates" | Münchmeyer 2024 | Location stage (`depth_pileup`, `depth_layers`) |
| Recovery of a routine or analyst catalog | Recall of an independent catalog within a time window; precision is low by construction when the new catalog is larger | GaMMA, 5 s window: recall 0.973 of SCSN (precision 0.336), > 0.95 for Liu et al. 2020 and Shelly 2020, 0.737 of the Ross et al. TM catalog. EQTransformer includes "almost all" JMA events. QTM adds back template events it failed to re-detect so that it is "an exact superset of the SCSN catalog" | Zhu et al. 2022; Mousavi et al. 2020; Ross et al. 2019 QTM | Reference-based; closest reference-free analogue is `energetic_windows` (largest transients must be in the catalog) |

### 1b. Detection (ML catalogs, template matching, similarity search)

| Criterion | What is checked and how | Typical thresholds / numbers | Source | Our check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Detection threshold in MAD units | Peak of the network-stacked CC (migrated to origin time) above k × MAD of the daily stacked CC | QTM: 9.5 MAD, "chosen based on visual examination of thousands of detections", false-alarm rate ≈ 5 %; 12 MAD gives < 1 % and 853,764 instead of 1.81 M events. Neves et al. 12 MAD. Skoumal et al. 15 MAD on network-normalised CC ("~1 false positive" per random year at 40 sps, theoretically). EQcorrscan tutorial example 10 MAD and advises starting low and re-thresholding. Lellouch et al.: studies use 5–15 MAD | Ross et al. 2019 QTM supplement; Neves et al. 2023; Skoumal et al. 2015; EQcorrscan docs; Lellouch et al. 2021 | PLANNED (CC/MAD distribution) |
| False-positive base rate is not portable | False rate for a given k depends on bandwidth, window length, number of channels and number of templates | Single-station filtered white noise: worst case (f_max 1.6 Hz, 20 s window) exceeds 7 MAD after one day and 8 MAD after 100 days; maximum attainable MAD multiple 5–20 across configurations; for N templates the false rate is N/T_c, e.g. 100 white-noise templates give one > 8 MAD false match per day. Arrays are "much more resilient" because the moveout acts as a barcode, but coherent noise and templates containing noise (cars, glitches) add false matches | Muir et al. 2023 | MISSING (scale the expected false count with templates × channels × days) |
| Null test | Run the same detector on data that cannot contain matching events: time-reversed templates, filtered noise, or time-shifted data | Lellouch et al.: time-reversed (acausal) template over the full record, maximum CC 0.09, used to set the detection cut-off. Schaff 2010: per-template null distribution from time reversal, threshold for a desired false-alarm rate [snippet]. Muir et al.: filtered white noise as a lower bound | Lellouch et al. 2021; Schaff 2010 [snippet]; Muir et al. 2023 | MISSING (the project ran shifted-data null runs for Magna and Prague, `00_B_Research_Plan_Checker.md`, but no check) |
| Visual inspection of random samples | Human review of randomly drawn detections to estimate false-alarm rate and tune the threshold | QTM: thousands of random detections; FAST: threshold 0.19 "set by visually inspecting waveforms", 12 false detections identified visually as noise; GaMMA and EQTransformer show example waveforms of new events | Ross et al. 2019 QTM; Yoon et al. 2015; Zhu et al. 2022 | MISSING (not automatable; a proxy is a waveform stack or record section of a random sample, which a text-only model needs as numbers) |
| Template quality | SNR, channel count, distance range, source type of templates | QTM: SNR > 5 (4 s pre-P noise window), ≥12 template channels (≥4 stations), ≤50 km, P 2.5 s or S−P, S 4.0 s, 2–15 Hz. Neves et al.: SNR > 5 on ≥12 channels. EQcorrscan: keep templates with ≥5 stations, test SNR. Skoumal et al.: blasts used as templates "register an average of over 100 matches"; 60 blasts removed from 111 cataloged events | Ross et al. 2019 QTM; Neves et al. 2023; EQcorrscan docs; Skoumal et al. 2015 | PLANNED (detections per template) |
| Duplicates across templates | One event detected by many templates; final catalog unique within a window | QTM 2.0 s; Neves et al. overlapping windows; detections by far templates occur "due to random chance", which is why QTM merges by time only | Ross et al. 2019 QTM; Neves et al. 2023 | `duplicate_events` (final catalog) |
| Detections of distant events (decoy templates) | Include templates outside the target region; detections best matched by a distant template are removed | Neves et al.: templates up to ~50 km away (e.g. San Simeon area); separate P and S windows are "prone to false detections from local templates that correlate strongly with impulsive P or S arrivals of distant large earthquakes"; removed detections whose best template is > 20 km off the fault: 115,263 detections → 28,107 local | Neves et al. 2023 | MISSING |
| Location of detections | Detections inherit the template hypocentre until relocated | QTM: detections "generally very closely located" to the template, ~100–200 m; templates relocated first (template–template weights × 100 in GrowClust); only 38.7 % of detections relocated | Ross et al. 2019 QTM supplement | MISSING (share of events at exact template coordinates; template-family offsets) |
| Detection magnitudes | M_new = M_template + c log10(amplitude ratio); calibrate c on template pairs; robust statistics | QTM: peak amplitude ratio with c = 1, median or Bayesian posterior with Laplace likelihood (b = 0.33) and exponential prior truncated at M_template ("we do not anticipate detecting larger earthquakes"); "for a few percent of the events, the magnitudes were slightly biased" by noisy amplitudes. Neves et al.: principal-component amplitude fit, c = 0.828 calibrated on template pairs, only templates of one magnitude type (Md); peak ratio "can be easily affected by noise" | Ross et al. 2019 QTM supplement; Neves et al. 2023; Shelly et al. 2016 [via Neves] | PLANNED (detection magnitudes). MISSING: c calibrated on template pairs; share of detections larger than their template |
| Completeness gain and frequency–magnitude consistency | Mc and event count against the routine catalog; new events should extend the GR relation | QTM: 10× events, Mc 1.7 → 0.3 (GFT; MAXC 0.5–0.6 lower). Tan et al.: 900,058 events, ~10× INGV, Mc 2.3 → ~0.3. EQTransformer Tottori: 2.5× events, Mc 1.82 → 1.50 (MAXC). GaMMA Ridgecrest: 3.5× SCSN, new events "follow the Gutenberg–Richter" relation, "which suggests that they may be legitimate". QuakeFlow > 10×. | Day/night modulation of detection | Hour-of-day histogram, day/night ratio, minimum magnitude by hour | Tan et al.: ~50 % more events around midnight than noon (≥6 months after Norcia) and minimum ML ~0.3 lower at night, not visible in the routine catalog: smallest PhaseNet events are near the noise level and the threshold follows cultural noise. | Quarry blasts and explosions | Daytime and weekday excess, spatial clustering (Rq map), waveform discrimination | Skoumal et al.: ratio of > 5 Hz to 0.4–1 Hz amplitude on a horizontal component, normalised by the same ratio in the minute before, averaged over the 10 closest stations: typically < 1 for blasts (Rg) and > 5 for earthquakes. Gulia et al.: blast-contaminated volumes show working-hour, weekday, lunch-break patterns; excess daytime events disappear above a maximum explosion magnitude (M 2.5 in their example); aftershock guard: ≤20 % of a node's daytime events on one day. Renouard et al.: > 99 % of false events and 95 % of quarry blasts correctly classified operationally [abs] | Skoumal et al. 2015; Gulia et al. 2012; Renouard et al. 2021 [abs]; Linville et al. 2019 [abs] | MISSING |
| Detection after large events (coda, blind time) | Rate and minimum magnitude immediately after the largest events | Tan et al.: detection of small events "deteriorates immediately after the largest earthquakes when the seismic data are saturated by the mainshock and aftershock codas". Neves et al.: MF in 2–8 Hz "fails to detect early aftershocks in the first minutes" (first detection 99 s after the mainshock). Helmstetter et al. 2006: Mc(M, t) = M − 4.5 − 0.75 log10 t (t in days), so m 2 incomplete for 4.6 days after M 7 [via Hainzl]. Hainzl: apparent b drops for rates above ~100–200 events/day; blind time ~7–83 s; coda alone is too short to explain the incompleteness | Tan et al. 2021; Neves et al. 2023; Hainzl 2016 | `rate_after_largest` (report-only) |
| Temporal homogeneity (network, gaps) | Rate and Mc against station availability and data completeness | Network changes produce rate changes "by up to 40 %" (Habermann 1986, Tormann et al. 2010 [via Gulia]); Mc in central Italy changed 0.1–0.3 units with network build-up and upgrades. MISSING: rate against station count and data coverage |
| Out-of-network events | Retrieval and Mc outside the network | Benchmark: retrieval declines with distance and drops sharply at the associator's search boundary. Gulia et al.: Mc offshore 0.9 units higher than on land in central Italy; treat as separate catalogs | Puente et al. 2025; Gulia et al. 2012 | Location stage (`outside_network`) |
| Spatial and per-station detectability | Station-level detection probability against magnitude and distance (logistic regression), combined into Mc(x) | ML catalog Central Italy: median Mc(x) 1.6 → 0.5 (P), 1.7 → 0.5 (S); larger station-to-station variability than the routine catalog | Chung et al. 2026 | MISSING (per-station pick share against distance and magnitude) |

## 2. Attribution of anomalies

"Attributable?" grades: yes = a single reference-free test usually separates the causes; partly = the test narrows the causes but leaves a named confound; no = the literature does not separate the causes without external information or human waveform review.

| Anomaly | Candidate causes | Discriminating evidence or test | Attributable? Confounds | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Event rate drops (or fails to rise) right after the largest event | Coda masking and blind time (expected); picker saturation; associator capacity at high pick rates; data gap, clipping or telemetry loss at the mainshock; minimum-pick rule removing events with partial coverage | Magnitude dependence: only small events missing and Mc(t) following M − 4.5 − 0.75 log10 t points to incompleteness; all magnitudes missing points to data or pipeline. Picks present but unassociated → associator; picks absent → picker or data; station availability in the window | Partly. Coda versus associator saturation versus picker is confounded: PyOcto's author could not separate picker from associator on Iquique; Hainzl shows the incompleteness lasts far longer than the coda, so coda length alone is not a test | Hainzl 2016; Münchmeyer 2024; Tan et al. 2021; Neves et al. 2023 |

The gain depends on network density and on the routine catalog's Mc, so a range, not a threshold | Ross et al. 2019 QTM; Tan et al. 2021; Mousavi et al. 2020; Zhu et al. 2022; Neves et al. 2023 |

## 3. Key insights for an automated checker

- Published validation is mostly reference-based (recall of a routine catalog within a time window) or human (visual review of random detections, waveform examples, FMD). The reference-free tools that the literature does use are: null tests, decoy templates, pick-pattern consistency, magnitude dependence of rate changes, hour-of-day and Rq statistics, and waveform discriminants for blasts. Those are the ones a checker can automate.
- MAD multiples are not portable thresholds. The false count at k × MAD scales with bandwidth, window length, channels, templates and days (Muir et al.), and published choices range from 8 to 15 MAD with false rates measured, not assumed (QTM 9.5 MAD ≈ 5 %, 12 MAD < 1 %). A checker should report the CC/MAD distribution of detections next to a null-test distribution, and flag only when no null test was run or the measured false share exceeds a few percent.
- Associated share has no correct value; it moves with the picker threshold (92 % at a normal PhaseNet threshold, 34–46 % at 0.05). It is informative per station (bad stations), over time (bursts of unassociated picks = distant events or noise) and against picks per event. Keep `associated_share` report-only unless it is extreme, and add the per-station form.
- Magnitude dependence is the single most useful discriminator. Detection artefacts (coda, cultural noise, network changes) affect the smallest magnitudes and vanish above Mc; real rate changes and magnitude shifts do not. Every rate anomaly check should be computed above and below a magnitude cut.
- Confounds that no reference-free test resolves and that the checker should state rather than resolve: coda masking versus associator saturation versus picker after mainshocks; network densification versus true rate increase (temporary deployments follow activity); real early aftershocks versus coda ghosts; true doublets versus duplicates within ~2 s; small real events versus chance false events with few picks.
- `rate_after_largest` should be magnitude-aware: a drop of small events in the first hours after an M ≥ 5 is expected (Mc(M, t) = M − 4.5 − 0.75 log10 t), while a drop of events above that Mc(t) is the anomaly.
- `duplicate_events` matches practice (QTM 2.0 s, PyOcto deduplication), but a time-only rule also merges real doublets; adding pick overlap turns it into a split/duplicate attribution.
- `events_with_few_picks` (< 4 P) is close to REAL's defaults (≥3 P) but looser than PyOcto/benchmark practice (≥10 picks, ≥4 stations with P and S) and Tan et al. (≥4 P and ≥7 total). The literature's stronger criterion is stations with both P and S.
- The strongest single false-detection control for template matching in the literature is the decoy-template rule (Neves et al.), followed by the null test. Both are cheap to request from an Agent and produce numbers, which matters because GLM reads figures only through text summaries.
- Detection magnitudes need their own calibration (c fitted on template pairs, one magnitude type). A c of 1 and peak ratios are common but biased near noise; a detection larger than its template is suspicious under QTM's prior.
- Day/night and Rq are standard (Wiemer & Baer recommend Rq maps as "a routine part of any microseismicity study" [abs]) and are cheap from the catalog alone; they double as a noise-threshold indicator (night excess) and a blast indicator (day excess). Magna (Wasatch Front, mining nearby) is the case where this matters most.

Recommended additions, in priority order: (1) null test and CC/MAD distribution for template matching (planned check, add null comparison); (2) detections per template with hour-of-day of each template's detections; (3) decoy or best-template-distance check; (4) hour-of-day histogram and Rq above and below Mc; (5) daily rate against station count and data coverage, split by magnitude; (6) per-event share of closer silent stations; (7) stations with both P and S per event; (8) share of detections at the template position; (9) per-station associated share and pick probability against distance; (10) detection magnitude calibration (c from template pairs, share above template magnitude).
